# KN-61, requantising inputs to sixteen bits

`Engine.Stage.Requant` is row 13's S4, the work of `quantise_with` in
`crates/exact` at the scheme cpu-decode runs (16-bit codes, one power of two
per group of 32): what decision 41 (KN-41) checks, now computed. Each group's
power is the largest `k` that keeps its largest input times `2^k` within
32767; each input's code is the input times `2^k`, rounded half to even and
capped at 32767, with the input's sign as a 32-bit two's complement word;
`powers` gives each group's `k` as a u32 word, the exponent the Rust
returns, and 0 for a group of zeros.

`k` is found as the Rust finds it, from the bit lengths of the largest input
and of the limit, one step down if that overshoots. At a limit of 32767 the
step down never happens: a 15-bit value is never above it. The planted
defect below is in the rounding, which the rows do exercise.

## What Lean proves

| Theorem | For |
| --- | --- |
| `requant_codes_are_signed_16` | every input: each code is below 32768 or at least 2^32 - 32767 |
| `requant_power_is_word` | every group: its power is a u32 word |
| `requant_codes_row_0` to `_3`, `requant_powers_row_0` to `_3` | four groups evaluated by Lean: random values within 3, small values, zeros, and powers of two that need a negative power |

A group's 32 values are written as two lists of 16 joined by
`Engine.Word::joined`, which keeps the module's JSON within its nesting limit.
